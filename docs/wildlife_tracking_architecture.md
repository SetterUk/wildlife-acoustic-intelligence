# Architectural Extension: Integrating Bioacoustic Recognition with Acoustic Localization and Wildlife Tracking

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Scope

The current implementation of **AnimalCLAP** provides zero-shot species recognition and biological trait identification from vocalization signals. AnimalCLAP operates strictly on acoustic-semantic features: it maps an audio waveform to a semantic representation aligned with taxonomic text descriptions.

**AnimalCLAP alone does not provide physical spatial localization, spatial coordinates, or animal movement tracking.**

To achieve non-invasive physical localization and movement trajectory estimation in wildlife reserves, bioacoustic recognition must be coupled with multi-channel spatial acoustic array processing. This document outlines the physical principles, algorithmic architecture, and hardware topology required to bridge taxonomic recognition with real-world acoustic source localization.

---

## 2. Multi-Microphone Spatial Array Architecture

### 2.1 Sensor Topology
To localize vocalizing wildlife in two dimensions (ground plane) or three dimensions (canopy/flight), synchronized distributed sensor nodes or localized microphone arrays are deployed:

1. **Distributed Sensor Network (DSN):**
   - 4 or more GPS/PTP (Precision Time Protocol)-synchronized omnidirectional acoustic sensors positioned around forest perimeters (baseline: 100m – 500m).
   - Suitable for long-range, high-amplitude vocalizations (e.g., tiger roars, elephant rumbles, wolf howls).
2. **Compact Microphone Arrays:**
   - Tetrahedral, circular, or uniform linear arrays (ULA) with inter-microphone spacing $d = \frac{c}{2 f_{\max}}$ to avoid spatial aliasing (where $c \approx 343\text{ m/s}$ in ambient air).
   - Suitable for short-range directional bearing estimation (Angle of Arrival, AoA).

## 3. Time Difference of Arrival (TDOA) and GCC-PHAT

### 3.1 Generalized Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform (GCC-PHAT)
For a pair of spatially separated microphones $i$ and $j$, the received acoustic signals are:
$$x_i(t) = s(t - \tau_i) + n_i(t)$$
$$x_j(t) = \alpha s(t - \tau_j) + n_j(t)$$

The time difference of arrival is $\tau_{ij} = \tau_i - \tau_j$.

The Generalized Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform (GCC-PHAT) whitens the cross-power spectral density to preserve only phase information, making it robust against reverberant forest environments:
$$R_{ij}^{\text{PHAT}}(\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{X_i(f) X_j^*(f)}{|X_i(f) X_j^*(f)|} e^{j 2 \pi f \tau} df$$

The optimal time lag $\hat{\tau}_{ij}$ corresponds to the global peak:
$$\hat{\tau}_{ij} = \arg\max_\tau R_{ij}^{\text{PHAT}}(\tau)$$

## 4. Multilateration & 3D Spatial Localization

Each pair of microphones forms a hyperboloid of possible source locations:
$$\| \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{m}_i \| - \| \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{m}_j \| = c \cdot \hat{\tau}_{ij}$$
where $\mathbf{p} = [x, y, z]^T$ is the unknown animal position and $\mathbf{m}_i, \mathbf{m}_j$ are known microphone coordinates.

With $M \ge 4$ non-coplanar synchronized microphones, non-linear least squares optimization (e.g. Levenberg-Marquardt) or Steered Response Power with Phase Transform (SRP-PHAT) computes the global spatial coordinates:
$$\hat{\mathbf{p}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{p}} \sum_{i=1}^{M-1} \sum_{j=i+1}^M \left( (\|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{m}_i\| - \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{m}_j\|) - c \hat{\tau}_{ij} \right)^2$$

## 5. Temporal Filtering and Trajectory Tracking

Animals vocalize intermittently rather than continuously. Movement tracking requires state estimation across discrete vocalization events:

1. **State Vector:**
   $$\mathbf{x}_k = [x_k, y_k, z_k, \dot{x}_k, \dot{y}_k, \dot{z}_k]^T$$
2. **Motion Model (Constant Velocity with Process Noise):**
   $$\mathbf{x}_k = \mathbf{F} \mathbf{x}_{k-1} + \mathbf{w}_{k-1}$$
3. **Data Association:**
   Spatial coordinates generated by TDOA are matched to specific species tracks identified by AnimalCLAP embeddings using the Hungarian algorithm or Mahalanobis distance gating.
4. **Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) / Particle Filter:**
   Smooths noisy TDOA localization fixes into continuous wildlife movement paths across the landscape.

## 6. System Integration Pipeline

```
  Step 1: Multi-Channel Acoustic Stream (48 kHz, 24-bit PCM)
  Step 2: Signal Activity Detection (SAD) & Windowing (10s)
  Step 3: Branch A -> AnimalCLAP Feature Extraction -> Top Species Identity
  Step 4: Branch B -> GCC-PHAT Pairwise Delays -> Hyperbolic Multilateration -> [X, Y, Z]
  Step 5: Fuse [Species_ID + Confidence] with [X, Y, Z + Covariance]
  Step 6: Update Kalman Filter for Track ID: Species_ID_001
  Step 7: Render Live Reserve Map with Species Trajectories
```

By decoupling bioacoustic identity (AnimalCLAP) from spatial physics (GCC-PHAT / TDOA), the architecture preserves model modularity while delivering full-spectrum wildlife intelligence.
